Role of Generative AI (AI for Security, Not Autonomous Research). This paper is an AI-for-Security systems study that uses a large language model (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, version 1017) as a bounded component within a human-designed compiler-testing pipeline. Generative AI was also used for language polishing during manuscript preparation. This work is not autonomous "auto-research": the human authors conceived, implemented, and checked the research problem, the four-stage architecture, the Check IR query primitives, the target ISA specifications, the experimental protocol, the literature review, the figures, and all bug root-cause analyses.

Accuracy and Originality. The authors conducted the literature survey, selected and checked every citation, designed and executed all experiments, and manually audited every candidate bug before reporting it upstream and including it in the paper. Generative AI was used for editorial purposes in this manuscript, and all outputs were inspected by the authors to ensure accuracy and originality.

Methodology and Independent Validation. Within DeFenzz, the LLM has two bounded roles. In offline preparation, it extracts structured security invariants from segmented compiler manuals, developer comments, and historical security patches using chain-of-thought prompting and hybrid BM25/dense retrieval. It also maps invariants to controlling compiler branches through read-only static-analysis tools, and writes high-level Go check logic over our human-authored Check IR query primitives and target ISA specification tables (x86-64, AArch64, RISC-V64, and LoongArch64). In online directed fuzzing, agents synthesize and mutate small C/C++ test programs guided by compile-time branch traces and checker verdicts. These roles address two bottlenecks: defense contracts are scattered across prose manuals and patches, and triggering silent defense bugs requires coupled multi-statement programs that random mutators rarely produce. LLM output is never trusted as a bug verdict. Every invariant must cite an explicit anchor in official documentation, source comments, or a confirmed patch. Each synthesized checker implements either a static binary or assembly check or a dynamic sandboxed execution check over Check IR primitives. Every checker must pass two deterministic offline gates: Go type and AST checks, and validation on hardened versus unhardened reference builds. During online fuzzing, the compiled checkers deterministically test each compiled seed with zero LLM involvement in bug adjudication. The authors manually checked all 131 candidate findings, keeping 111 true bugs with minimal reproducers and excluding 20 non-bug cases.

Limitations and Reproducibility. Hosted LLM APIs are closed-weight and non-deterministic, so raw model outputs cannot be replayed bit-for-bit, and a rerun of DeFenzz may produce different invariants, checkers, and seeds. Our findings do not depend on replaying the model. Each of the 111 bugs comes with a minimal reproducer filed upstream, which anyone can check with a stock compiler and no model calls. Our anonymized artifact releases the full source code, including the Check IR oracle engine, the agent prompts, and the pipeline configurations, together with an anonymized catalog of all 111 bugs.

Responsibility and Resource Efficiency. We did not train or fine-tune any models or collect personal data. All model inputs come from public GCC and LLVM source code, public ISA and ABI manuals, and synthesized C/C++ test programs. To limit computational and environmental cost, we chose a compact Flash-tier model (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash). Offline preparation (38,256 calls, 709.2M tokens, 34.52 h) runs once per compiler release. Prompts are restricted to single-function or per-option slices and top-k retrieved items, and fixed schema and Check IR prefixes give a 92.4% KV-cache hit rate (1,427.4M of 1,610.0M total tokens across 59,671 calls). Compile-time probe injection and online bug adjudication need no LLM calls, which bounds the full four-ISA online campaign to 21,415 calls (10.16 h) on a single Linux server (AMD Ryzen 7 9700X CPU, 32 GB RAM).
